Prefill vs decode

Prefill is compute-bound: large matrix multiplies over every prompt token, so it drives TTFT. Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: every step reads all the weights to produce one token per sequence. Batching many sequences shares that read, which is why throughput rises with concurrency.

Weights
VRAM_weights = params × bytes/param

FP16/BF16 = 2 · FP8 = 1 · INT4 = 0.5 bytes. For MoE, count total params, not active ones.

KV cache
KV/token = 2 × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × bytes

Multiply by (concurrent sequences × context length). This is what limits concurrency. GQA (fewer KV heads) and an FP8 KV cache shrink it.

Decode speed ceiling (batch 1)
tok/s ≲ memory bandwidth ÷ weight bytes

A100 80 GB at ~2 TB/s with 16 GB of weights gives a ceiling of ~120 tok/s per sequence. That's why quantization speeds up decode.

Little's Law (capacity)
in-flight = arrival rate × latency

10 req/s × 4 s = 40 concurrent sequences. That number sizes your KV cache.

Cost, GPU-only
$/1M tok = GPU-hrs × $/GPU-hr ÷ (out tok / 1M)

Compare only at matched quality and matched P95 SLO. If you don't have a sourced price, leave the cost as N/A.

Worked sizing · Llama-3.1-8B, FP16

weights 8B × 2 B≈ 16 GB
KV/token 2×32×8×128×2 B= 128 KiB
32 users × 8K context= 262K tokens
KV cache 262K × 128 KiB≈ 32 GiB
total + runtime overhead≈ 50 GB

Fits an A100/H100 80 GB with headroom. Doesn't fit a 48 GB L40S as-is: cut concurrency or context, or use FP8 weights + KV.
